# Naqla business model

## Business concept

Naqla (نقلة) is a proposed platform for organizing home moves. Customers plan their move, inventory belongings, compare service providers, book an appointment, and follow delivery. Moving companies and their technicians receive structured job details and manage execution.

**Initial market assumption:** Start in one Saudi city, using Arabic-first customer and technician experiences. The launch city, demand, pricing, and legal requirements still need validation.

## Business model canvas

| Element | Proposed model |
| --- | --- |
| Customer segments | Households moving within one city; initially renters and families who need packing and transport. Moving companies are supply-side partners and potential future software customers. |
| Value proposition | A clear moving plan, reviewed inventory, comparable quotes, reliable scheduling, and visible job progress in one place. Providers receive better job information and access to bookings. |
| Channels | Search, social media, referrals, moving-company partnerships, and relationships with property managers and real estate agents. |
| Customer relationships | Guided booking, reminders, support during the move, documented issue resolution, and post-service reviews. |
| Revenue streams | A commission on completed moving bookings. Later options include packing add-ons, partner referral fees, and provider software subscriptions. |
| Key activities | Recruit and verify providers, match requests, coordinate bookings, support active moves, resolve issues, and maintain the platform. |
| Key resources | Product and engineering, provider network, operations and support staff, booking and inventory data, and brand trust. |
| Key partners | Moving companies, their technicians, payment providers, packing suppliers, and property partners. Utility and internet partners may be added later. |
| Cost structure | Product development, hosting, customer acquisition, payment processing, support, provider onboarding, refunds, and claims handling. |

## Customers and service providers

### Customer needs

- Understand what needs to be moved and how to prepare.
- Get a quote that reflects belongings, access conditions, distance, and required services.
- Book a dependable appointment and receive clear status updates.
- Know who to contact when a provider is late or an item is damaged.

### Provider needs

- Receive qualified requests with addresses, inventory, access notes, and appointment times.
- Assign technicians and equipment appropriate to the job.
- Track execution and document delivery.
- Understand fees, payout timing, cancellations, and dispute handling before accepting work.

For the initial model, moving companies supply the vehicles and moving teams. Naqla coordinates the marketplace; owning a fleet is outside the initial launch scope. The contractual relationship and responsibilities must be agreed before launch.

## Service and transaction workflow

1. **Request:** The customer enters pickup and destination details, moving date, inventory, and any packing or access requirements.
2. **Quote:** Eligible providers supply quotes against the same request. The customer sees the scope, exclusions, total price, and cancellation terms before choosing.
3. **Confirmation:** A provider confirms availability and assigns a team. The customer accepts the quote and completes the supported payment or deposit step.
4. **Execution:** The technician reviews the job, records collection, updates transport progress, and records delivery.
5. **Completion:** The customer confirms completion or reports an issue. Both sides can provide evidence when something is disputed.
6. **Settlement:** Payment is reconciled through the selected payment provider. Naqla records its commission and the provider’s payout according to agreed terms.
7. **Review:** The customer rates the service; Naqla tracks provider quality and follows up on unresolved issues.

Payment methods, payout timing, deposits, cancellation charges, damage responsibility, and refund rules are business decisions to finalize with providers and payment/legal advisers. Do not promise escrow or insurance until supported arrangements exist.

## Revenue strategy

| Revenue stream | Launch priority | How it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Completed-booking commission | Primary | Naqla earns an agreed percentage of the eligible booking value after a move is completed. |
| Packing and materials | Optional pilot add-on | Earn a disclosed commission or margin on selected packing services and supplies. |
| Home-service referrals | Later | Earn an agreed referral fee for eligible internet, utility, or other partner conversions. |
| Provider subscription | Later, subject to demand | Charge providers for scheduling, reporting, or team-management tools once their value is demonstrated. |

Start with one clear revenue stream. Avoid charging a provider both a subscription and a booking commission before validating that the combined value justifies the fees.

## Illustrative unit economics

The figures below are planning assumptions in Saudi riyals (SAR), not market research, approved prices, or a financial forecast. They exclude VAT and require tax/accounting review. The example assumes the commission is deducted from the provider’s booking value, with no additional customer platform fee.

| Per completed move | Example |
| --- | ---: |
| Booking value | SAR 1,000 |
| Naqla commission rate | 12% |
| Naqla commission revenue | SAR 120 |
| Provider share before its own costs | SAR 880 |
| Payment processing cost borne by Naqla | SAR 25 |
| Variable support cost | SAR 15 |
| Refund/claims cost allowance | SAR 10 |
| Contribution before acquisition and fixed costs | SAR 70 |
| Customer acquisition cost allocated to the booking | SAR 40 |
| Contribution after acquisition, before fixed costs | SAR 30 |

**Formula:** Contribution per completed booking = commission revenue + other earned fees − payment costs − variable support − refunds/claims allowance − allocated acquisition cost.

Booking value is gross merchandise value (GMV); it is not Naqla’s revenue. Actual revenue recognition depends on the final contractual model. The claims allowance is a budgeting assumption, not insurance coverage.

At an illustrative SAR 30,000 in monthly fixed costs and SAR 30 contribution per booking, operating break-even would require **1,000 completed bookings per month**. This is a sensitivity example, not a launch target; lower margins or higher acquisition costs increase the required volume.

## Launch plan

### 1. Validate the problem and supply

- Interview customers who recently moved and providers serving the proposed launch city.
- Test whether customers prefer instant estimates, provider quotes, or a managed booking service.
- Confirm provider capacity, pricing factors, required documents, and service coverage.
- Agree commission, payment, cancellation, and dispute terms before accepting paid bookings.

### 2. Run a focused pilot

- Limit service to one city and clearly defined moving services.
- Recruit a small group of verified providers with agreed service standards.
- Support inventory entry, quote comparison, booking, technician status updates, and completion reporting.
- Use manual operations where appropriate to learn how matching and support should work.
- Keep AI room scanning optional with customer review; do not depend on unvalidated detection for binding quotes.

### 3. Expand after evidence

Add providers, neighborhoods, packing services, and integrations only when completion rates, service quality, and contribution justify expansion. Consider utility referrals and provider subscriptions after the core moving workflow is reliable.

## Metrics to track

| Metric | What it tells us |
| --- | --- |
| Request-to-booking conversion | Whether qualified requests turn into confirmed bookings. |
| Provider response and acceptance time | Whether supply can respond quickly enough. |
| Completion and cancellation rates | Whether confirmed bookings result in successful moves. |
| On-time collection and delivery | Whether providers meet the agreed service window. |
| Complaint, damage, and refund rates | Whether quality and issue handling are sustainable. |
| GMV, earned revenue, and effective take rate | Transaction volume and how much Naqla earns from it. |
| Contribution per completed booking | Whether bookings cover their variable and acquisition costs. |
| Customer acquisition cost by channel | Which channels bring economical bookings. |
| Referral bookings and customer satisfaction | Whether customers trust and recommend the service. |

Moving is infrequent for many households, so the model should not rely on frequent repeat purchases without evidence. Referrals and property partnerships may matter more than short-term repeat bookings.

## Main risks and responses

| Risk | Proposed response |
| --- | --- |
| Unreliable or insufficient supply | Verify providers, confirm capacity, monitor performance, and define a fallback process. |
| Quote disputes or unexpected charges | Collect reviewed inventory and access details; require customer approval for scope changes. |
| Damage or missing items | Record handover evidence, define responsibilities, and establish a claims process before launch. |
| Off-platform transactions | Provide useful booking records, support, transparent fees, and convenient payments. |
| High acquisition costs | Measure channel economics and test referrals and property partnerships. |
| Sensitive room photos and addresses | Limit collection and access, define retention/deletion rules, and obtain appropriate consent. |
| Regulatory or payment constraints | Validate Saudi business, transport, consumer, data-protection, tax, and payment requirements with qualified advisers. |

## Decisions still needed

- Launch city and first customer segment.
- Provider eligibility and verification process.
- Quote format, commission rate, and which party bears payment fees.
- Payment flow, payouts, cancellations, refunds, and damage responsibilities.
- Pilot budget, staffing, and measurable launch criteria.
- Which features belong in the first release versus later phases.
